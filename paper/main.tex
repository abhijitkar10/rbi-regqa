\begin{abstract}
In November 2025 the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) replaced 9,445 circulars with 244 consolidated \emph{Directions}, one set per type of lender. Many of these are near-copies: the Know-Your-Customer (KYC) rules for small finance banks, payments banks and four other bank types repeat \NearCopyBankRange{} of the commercial-bank paragraphs almost word for word. A compliance assistant must therefore find not just the right rule, but the right lender's version of it, and cite the paragraph. We release \textbf{RBI-RegQA}, \NQuestions{} questions over \NDocs{} RBI Directions (\NParas{} numbered paragraphs), each tied to the paragraph that answers it, plus \NUnans{} questions the rules do not answer. Questions were drafted by two LLM families from single paragraphs, kept only when an evidence quote matched the source text, and audited by hand. With plain text chunks, the best retriever returns the right rule from the wrong lender's rulebook as its top result for \FixedWL{} of questions; naming the rulebook in each paragraph chunk cuts this to \TitleWLRange{} across dense, hybrid and re-ranked retrievers and raises R@5 from \FixedRfive{} to \BestRfive{}. Across \NReaders{} open readers, accuracy is \RdGoldIn{} when the gold paragraph is in the context and \RdGoldOut{} when it is not. On the author's own questions, which rarely name a lender, R@1 is only \ExpertRone{}; defaulting to the deployment's lender raises it to \LAExpertRone{}. Data and code: \url{https://github.com/abhijitkar10/rbi-regqa}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Banks in India answer to rules written by the RBI. Until 2025 these were spread across thousands of circulars, guidelines and Master Directions. In November 2025 the RBI repealed 9,445 circulars and replaced them with 244 Master Directions, each written for one of 11 types of regulated entity \citep{rbi2025consolidation}: commercial banks, small finance banks, payments banks, local area banks, regional rural banks, urban and rural co-operative banks, non-banking financial companies (NBFCs) and others. The consolidation made the rules easier to find for a human who knows which rulebook applies. It made them harder for a retrieval system, because the same rule now appears many times with small, consequential differences.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is the usual way to build a question-answering assistant over such text. Prior regulatory QA work built a large synthetic benchmark over Abu Dhabi financial regulations \citep{gokhan2024rirag}; legal benchmarks cover case law and statutes \citep{pipitone2024legalbenchrag,joshi2024iltur}. None tests the situation the RBI consolidation creates: a corpus where the answer exists, nearly verbatim, in several rulebooks, and only one of them is correct for the question asked.

We make three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RBI-RegQA}, an open benchmark of \NQuestions{} answerable and \NUnans{} unanswerable questions over \NDocs{} RBI Directions, each answerable question tied to a numbered paragraph and an evidence quote that a script checks against the source.
\item A measurement of \textbf{near-copy confusion}: how often retrievers return the right rule from the wrong lender's rulebook, under three chunking strategies, four embedding models, hybrid fusion and two re-rankers, and two cheap fixes: naming the rulebook in each chunk, and re-ordering results by the lender the question names.
\item An evaluation of \NReaders{} open models answering with paragraph citations, including whether they refuse unanswerable questions, and a check of whether a model family scores higher on questions its own family drafted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Regulatory and legal QA.} RIRAG \citep{gokhan2024rirag} introduced ObliQA, 27,869 synthetic questions over 40 regulatory documents from Abu Dhabi Global Market, validated with natural-language inference. LegalBench-RAG \citep{pipitone2024legalbenchrag} evaluates retrieval of exact legal snippets. IL-TUR \citep{joshi2024iltur} benchmarks Indian legal text understanding over court judgments. Commercial legal research tools still hallucinate \citep{magesh2024hallucination}. The only Indian banking-regulation resource we found is a 60-question extractive set with the passage given \citep{jain2026bfsi}; it involves no retrieval.

\paragraph{Near-duplicate documents.} SEC filings share boilerplate, and HiREC retrieves documents before passages to keep near-duplicates apart \citep{choe2025hirec}. Prefixing chunks with document metadata is common practice; a recent study on regulatory filings finds it consistently beats plain text \citep{yousuf2026metadata}. Our name prefix is an instance of this. We measure the specific confusion it fixes, the right rule from the wrong entity's rulebook, and show that it remains when the question does not name the entity.

\paragraph{Unanswerable questions and citations.} SQuAD~2.0 showed that systems must learn to abstain \citep{rajpurkar2018squad2}. ALCE evaluates whether generated citations support the text \citep{gao2023alce}. We check citations at paragraph level and score refusals separately.

\paragraph{LLM-generated benchmarks.} LLM judges are convenient but biased \citep{zheng2023judge}, and models favour their own generations \citep{panickssery2024selfpref}. We therefore draft questions with two model families, test whether readers from the same family score higher on them, report an expert-written subset separately, and audit a random sample by hand.

\section{The RBI-RegQA Benchmark}

\paragraph{Corpus.} We fetched every Commercial Bank and NBFC Direction listed on the RBI website, plus the KYC Directions of every other lender type, \NDocs{} documents in all, fetched on \SnapshotDate{}. The Directions were issued in November 2025; \NUpdated{} of them have since been amended, with latest amendments dated from \UpdateRange{}. The RBI's PDF host blocks automated downloads with a script check, so we used the HTML version of each Direction, which keeps the paragraph numbering. A parser splits each Direction into its numbered paragraphs (\NParas{} in total). The RBI website reserves its rights, so we release the questions, answers, paragraph identifiers, short evidence quotes and code, not the Direction texts: a script fetches them, and per-paragraph fingerprints of our snapshot show which paragraphs, and which questions, a later amendment affects.

\input{tab_nearcopy}

\paragraph{Near-copies.} For every paragraph of each other lender's KYC Directions we find the most similar paragraph of the Commercial Banks KYC Directions by word-trigram Jaccard similarity (Table~\ref{tab:nearcopy}). For the six other bank types, \NearCopyBankRange{} of paragraphs are near-identical ($\geq0.9$). The NBFC, AIFI and ARC Directions are mostly edited copies: the same rule with the lender's own terms, thresholds or exemptions substituted. These edited paragraphs are where the right rulebook changes the answer, so we sample them separately when drafting questions.

\paragraph{Question drafting.} We sampled up to 16 paragraphs from each of 19 source Directions and \NEntityParas{} paragraphs where another lender's KYC text differs from the commercial-bank text. Two generators from different families, \GenA{} and \GenB{}, each drafted up to two questions per paragraph with a short answer, an evidence quote and a type, plus one question on the same topic that the paragraph does not answer. Prompts are in Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}.

\paragraph{Verification.} A question is kept only if every part of its evidence quote appears, in order, in the source paragraph (after normalising whitespace, hyphens and list markers; quotes may elide with an ellipsis). \QuoteOkGenA{} of \GenA{}'s and \QuoteOkGenB{} of \GenB{}'s quotes passed. The benchmark keeps one question per paragraph, alternating generators. In a hand audit of \AuditN{} random questions, the author judged \AuditFair{} fair and \AuditCorrect{} reference answers correct.

\paragraph{Unanswerable questions.} A drafted unanswerable question is kept only if a verifier model, shown the top-5 passages our strongest retriever finds in the whole corpus, says none of them answers it. In a hand audit of \AuditUnN{} random kept questions, \AuditUnTrue{} were truly unanswerable and \AuditUnFair{} were fair questions on the topic.

\paragraph{Expert subset.} The author's 50 hand-written questions from an earlier KYC system \citep{kycrag} were re-anchored to the 2025 Commercial Banks KYC Directions; \NExpertAnchored{} could be tied to a verified paragraph.

\paragraph{Customer wording.} Generated questions reuse the rulebook's vocabulary, which flatters keyword retrieval. We therefore rewrite every question the way an ordinary customer would ask it, keeping the facts, and report results on both wordings.

\section{Experiments}

\paragraph{Retrieval.} We compare three chunkings: fixed 800-character windows with 200 overlap; one chunk per numbered paragraph (long paragraphs split at 1,500 characters); and the same paragraph chunks prefixed with the rulebook name. Retrievers: BM25 \citep{robertson2009bm25}; dense retrieval with BGE-small and BGE-base \citep{xiao2023cpack}, BGE-M3 \citep{chen2024m3} and Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B \citep{zhang2025qwen3emb}; reciprocal rank fusion of BM25 and dense \citep{cormack2009rrf}; and two cross-encoder re-rankers over the fused top 30. A retrieved chunk is a hit if it comes from the gold Direction and covers the gold paragraph. We also report \emph{WrongLender@1}, the share of questions whose top chunk is on the right topic but from another lender's rulebook. Differences between configurations come with paired bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals over questions (10,000 resamples).

\paragraph{Answering.} \NReaders{} open models answer from the top-5 passages of name-prefixed paragraphs retrieved by hybrid BM25 + BGE-base and re-ranked by BGE-reranker-base (the gold paragraph is among them for \CtxRfive{} of questions; the configurations were still being compared when answering began). Two readers also answer from the best configuration's top 5 (\CtxBestRfive{}) to measure what better retrieval buys. Readers cite passages as [S1]--[S5] or reply NOT FOUND. An LLM judge grades answers against the reference; a second judge from another family grades a sample to measure agreement. A script checks each cited passage against the gold paragraph.

\section{Results}

\input{tab_retrieval}

\paragraph{The rulebook name matters more than the retriever.} Table~\ref{tab:retrieval} shows retrieval on the \NQuestions{} answerable questions. With plain text chunks, every retriever returns the right rule from the wrong lender's rulebook for a large share of questions: \FixedWL{} of top-1 results for the best configuration on 800-character windows, and \ParaWLRange{} on paragraph chunks. Prefixing each paragraph chunk with its rulebook's name (e.g.\ ``Small Finance Banks -- Know Your Customer, para 18'') cuts this to \TitleWLRange{} for dense, hybrid and re-ranked retrieval (Figure~\ref{fig:wl}), and raises the share of top-1 results from the correct rulebook from \FixedBook{} to \BestBook{}. The best configuration (name-prefixed paragraphs, hybrid BM25 + Qwen3-Embedding, BGE-reranker-v2-m3) reaches R@1 \BestRone{} and R@5 \BestRfive{}, against R@5 \FixedRfive{} for the best configuration without names (paired 95\% CI of the gain: \CIPrefixRfive{}; of the drop in WrongLender@1: \CIPrefixWL{} points). BM25 benefits least: a lender name is one or two tokens against dozens shared by the near-copies.

\paragraph{Paragraphs alone do not help.} Without the name prefix, paragraph chunks retrieve slightly worse than overlapping fixed windows. Overlapping windows carry neighbouring headings and context; a bare paragraph is often indistinguishable from its near-copies.

\paragraph{Applicability questions are hardest.} For the best configuration, questions about whether a rule applies to a given entity or transaction reach R@1 \AppRone{} and R@5 \AppRfive{} (\NApp{} questions), against \OblRone{} and \OblRfive{} for obligations. Questions drafted by \GenB{} are harder to retrieve than those drafted by \GenA{} (R@5 \GenBRfive{} vs \GenARfive{}), so the choice of generator shifts a synthetic benchmark's difficulty; mixing two generators keeps either from setting it alone.

\paragraph{Customer wording is much harder, and the re-ranker earns its cost there.} On the \NCust{} questions whose plain-language paraphrase kept the lender type, the best configuration falls from R@5 \CustOffRfive{} on the original wording to \CustRfive{} (95\% CI of the drop: \CICustRfive{}), and BM25 on the same chunks from \CustBMOff{} to \CustBM{}: customers do not use the rulebook's vocabulary. The cross-encoder adds \OffRerankGain{} R@5 over its hybrid input on rulebook wording but \CustRerankGain{} on customer wording (difference \RerankDiff{}, 95\% CI \CIRerankDiff{}). The lost questions are not near-copy confusions (WrongLender@1 stays at \CustWL{}); the top result is from the correct rulebook for only \CustBook{} of questions, against \CustOffBook{}, because the paraphrase drifts to a different topic.

\paragraph{Real questions rarely name the lender.} The author's hand-written questions were written for a single KYC rulebook, and \NExpertUnnamed{} of the \NExpertQ{} anchored ones never say which kind of lender they concern. Scored strictly against the Commercial Banks rulebook, the name-prefixed configuration with BGE-reranker-v2-m3 reaches R@1 \ExpertRone{} and R@5 \ExpertRfive{}, and for \ExpertWL{} of questions the top result is another lender's copy. If a near-identical copy (Jaccard $\geq0.9$) in any lender's KYC rulebook counts as a hit, R@1 rises to \ExpertAnyRone{} and R@5 to \ExpertAnyRfive{}. The retriever finds the rule but cannot know whose rule the user needs; an assistant has to ask, or infer the lender from context, before it cites a paragraph.

\input{tab_lender}

\paragraph{Knowing the lender removes most of the confusion.} A keyword match finds a lender type in \LANamed{} rulebook-wording questions, and it is the gold rulebook's lender for \LAPrec{} of them. Moving that lender's rulebooks to the front of the retrieved list (Table~\ref{tab:lender}) cuts WrongLender@1 on plain 800-character windows from \LAFixedWL{} to \LAFixedWLp{} and raises R@1 from \LAFixedRone{} to \LAFixedRonep{}. It does not replace the name prefix: R@5 reaches only \LAFixedRfivep{}, likely because the prefix also names the rulebook's topic, which helps pick the right Direction among the same lender's dozens. On prefixed chunks the re-ordering adds little. Only \LAExpertNamed{} expert questions name a lender, so re-ordering by the question alone barely helps them; an assistant that knows whose customers it serves can default to that lender, which lifts R@1 from \ExpertRone{} to \LAExpertRone{}, close to the \LAExpertRthirty{} ceiling of the top 30. The expert gold is the Commercial Banks rulebook by construction, so this measures what deployment context buys, not a fairer retriever.

\input{tab_misses}

\paragraph{The remaining misses are mostly the wrong paragraph of the right rulebook.} Table~\ref{tab:misses} sorts every question the best configuration misses at R@5. Once chunks carry their rulebook's name, near-copy confusion is rare: \MissNearCopy{} of \MissAll{} misses. Most misses (\MissOffRight{} of \MissOff{} on rulebook wording, \MissCustRight{} of \MissCust{} on customer wording) retrieve the correct rulebook but another paragraph, usually from the same section: for \MissOffNeighbour{} of the \MissOffRight{} rulebook-wording cases, a paragraph within three numbers of the gold one is in the top five. In a typical case, a question on how a bank must monitor exporters' use of pre-shipment credit has the end-use paragraph as gold, and the retriever ranks the next paragraph, on exporting the final product, first. Some misses are artefacts of single-paragraph labels: a customer asking who is responsible when a bank's outsourced recovery agents misbehave gets, at rank 1, a paragraph from another Commercial Banks Direction stating that ``the bank, as principal, is responsible for the actions of its agents'', which the gold label scores as a miss. \MissBoiler{} misses have a boilerplate gold paragraph found in ten or more rulebooks, such as the clause that a Direction adds to other laws rather than replacing them. On customer wording, \MissCustOffTopic{} misses drift to another topic altogether.

\input{tab_readers}

\paragraph{Readers answer well when the paragraph reaches them.} Table~\ref{tab:readers} shows the \NReaders{} readers. Accuracy ranges over \RdAccRange{}, with MiniMax-M3.1 highest (\RdTopAcc{}); every reader cites the gold paragraph in \RdCiteRange{} of its answers and wrongly refuses \RdFalseRefRange{} of answerable questions. Averaged over readers, accuracy is \RdGoldIn{} when the gold paragraph is among the five passages and \RdGoldOut{} for the \NGoldOut{} questions where it is not, so retrieval sets the ceiling. Applicability questions are hardest to answer as well as to retrieve (\RdApp{} against \RdObl{} for obligations). Wrong-lender answers are rare: at most \FailWLMax{} per reader (Appendix~\ref{app:failures}), because the name prefix usually keeps the other lender's copy out of the top five.

\paragraph{Better retrieval buys accuracy.} With the best configuration's top five, which contain the gold paragraph for \CtxBestRfive{} of questions instead of \CtxRfive{}, Nemotron Bolt rises from \BoltAcc{} to \BoltAccBest{} and Nemotron Lightning from \LightAcc{} to \LightAccBest{}, with citation scores of \BoltCiteBest{} and \LightCiteBest{}.

\paragraph{Refusal depends on how it is scored.} Under the strict rule (the reply starts with NOT FOUND), readers refuse \OtherUnStrictRange{} of unanswerable questions, except Qwen3.8-27B at \QwenUnStrict{}. Qwen usually declines in its own words (``the provided passages do not specify\ldots''); counting such replies raises it to \QwenUnSoft{}, and it accounts for \FailHedgeQwen{} of these hedges against at most \FailHedgeOtherMax{} for any other reader. A pipeline that routes on an exact refusal string would pass Qwen's hedges to users as answers. The lenient score also counts empty replies, of which Nemotron Lightning returned \LightEmpty{} (\LightEmptyBest{} with the best context).

\paragraph{No clear own-family preference.} Questions drafted by \GenB{} are harder for every reader, including MiniMax-M3.1, so raw accuracy cannot show self-preference. We compare each reader's accuracy gap between the two generators' questions with the gap of the readers from other families: Nemotron readers gain \PrefNem{} on Nemotron-drafted questions (95\% bootstrap CI \PrefNemCI{}) and MiniMax-M3.1 \PrefMM{} on its own (\PrefMMCI{}). Neither interval excludes zero.

\paragraph{The judges agree moderately.} On \JudgeN{} answers graded by both Step-5 and gpt-oss-120b, the grades match for \JudgeExact{} of them (Cohen's $\kappa$ = \JudgeKappa{}). A third judge, Mistral Large 4, agrees with Step-5 about as well ($\kappa$ = \JudgeThreeKappa{}, $n$ = \JudgeThreeN{}) but less with gpt-oss-120b ($\kappa$ = \JudgeOthersKappa{}, $n$ = \JudgeOthersN{}), so Step-5 sits between the other two rather than off to one side.

\section{Conclusion}
RBI-RegQA tests retrieval over a corpus where the same rule appears in many lenders' rulebooks. Three findings stand out. Retrievers often return the right rule from the wrong lender's rulebook, and stronger retrievers barely change this; writing the rulebook's name into each chunk cuts WrongLender@1 to \TitleWLRange{} for every retriever but BM25. Once the paragraph reaches them, readers answer well (\RdGoldIn{} against \RdGoldOut{} without it), so retrieval sets the ceiling. And real questions rarely name the lender, so even a perfect retriever cannot know which copy the user needs. For RAG over versioned or per-entity rulebooks, we recommend putting the document's identity into every chunk and deciding the entity before retrieval, from the question or from deployment context, rather than leaving the retriever to guess.

\section*{Limitations}
Questions are drafted from single paragraphs, so the benchmark under-represents questions that need several rules at once. Each question has one gold paragraph, so a second paragraph that also answers it counts as a miss (see the discussion of Table~\ref{tab:misses}); our retrieval scores are therefore lower bounds. Two RBI Directions parse poorly and are used only as distractors. The RBI updates Directions often; the benchmark is a snapshot of \SnapshotDate{}. Judges and verifiers are LLMs; we report their agreement with each other and with a human sample rather than assume it. On the reader side, one LLM judge grades every answer, and it agrees only moderately with a second ($\kappa$ = \JudgeKappa{}), so differences of a few points between readers are within judge noise. The lenient refusal score counts empty replies as refusals. Most readers saw the context of a weaker retriever than our best one, and only the two Nemotron readers were rerun with the best context. Each reader answered once at its default sampling settings, so we do not measure run-to-run variance.
